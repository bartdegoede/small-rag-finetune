# v1 results: fine-tuning on dataset v1

Date: 2026-10-16. Plan: `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-16-plan3-train-eval.md`.

## Summary

The v1 fine-tune does not beat the base models on answerable questions, and with
the blog's decoding settings it makes Qwen3-0.6B worse. It does learn to refuse:
with no repetition penalty, the fine-tuned Qwen3 refuses all 15 questions the blog
doesn't cover. Most of its errors on answerable questions trace back to two habits
it learned from the data, both fixable without new API spend (see "Next").

A side finding matters for the blog itself: the page's `repetition_penalty: 1.15`
hurts base Qwen3-0.6B. Without it, the base model scores 17/30 instead of 13/30.

The blog's 45 questions, judged by the blog's `judge.py` (Opus 5.5). Every arm is
exported the same way (`scripts/export_onnx.py`, q4f16) and run with

## What goes wrong

**SmolLM2-360M** learned the format (two sentences, refusals, hedges) but not the
reading: it garbles passages, e.g. "30B parameters" becomes "30B tokens". 360M is
too small for this task; drop it.

**Qwen3-0.6B v1** (penalty 1.0), the 22 answerable questions it got wrong:

- **Wrong partial hedges, 6.** "The blog doesn't explain X" when the passages do
  explain X (q06, q14, q21, q23, q25, q27). 10.4% of training answers (472 of
  4,529) end in such a hedge; the fine-tune adds one to 10 of 30 answers, the base
  model to none. Opus adds a hedge when something really is missing. The 0.6B
  model learns to append the clause anyway.
- **Over-refusal, 4.** It says the blog doesn't cover a question the passages do
  answer (q04, q12, q17, q30).
- **Garbled details, 12.** Facts from different passages get merged or reversed
  ("King and bicycle are close together"; the agent's description attributed to
  the model). The base model makes fewer of these because its answers are vaguer:
  it repeats less, so it has less to get wrong.

Example, q10 ("Which model does the local coding agent run?"):

- base, penalty 1.15 (faithful): "The local coding agent uses Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B."
- v1, penalty 1.15 (unsupported): "The local coding agent is
  **Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B**, a "mixture-of-experts" model with 30B parameters over
  ±3B activation per token. It is tuned for multi-step tool use, editing files,
  and running bash, as well as being a minimal agent that reads, writes and
  edits, and runs bash."

**Repetition penalty.** transformers.js applies the penalty to every token in the
sequence, prompt included. That pushes the model away from the exact words it
should copy from the passages. It hurts most for the fine-tune, which was trained
to copy them.

## Next

v2, no new teacher calls:

1. Remove the partial hedges from the teacher answers: drop the hedge sentence
   when the rest of the answer stands on its own, and drop the example otherwise.
2. Train Qwen3-0.6B only, for 1 epoch (the best checkpoint was at epoch 1 anyway):
   about 1h20m on the M4.
3. Evaluate at repetition penalty 1.0, against base Qwen3-0.6B at 1.0 (17/30).

Separately, for the blog: re-run the blog's own eval with penalty 1.0 for each
model before changing `assets/js/ask/models.js`. The penalty was added to stop
small models from looping, which this eval doesn't measure.

## v2: without partial-answer hedges (same day)

`small-rag-finetune strip-hedges` removed the hedge sentence or clause from 578
teacher answers and dropped 62 examples that led with one (`data/v2`: train
4,477, valid 248, test 257). Qwen3-0.6B, 1 epoch, otherwise as v1
(`scripts/train_v2.sh`, 1h18m). Validation loss fell at every checkpoint: 0.804,
0.778, 0.760, 0.740.

All arms at repetition penalty 1.0:

| Qwen3-0.6B | faithful (of 30) | non-answer | invented (of 15) |
|---|---|---|---|
| base | 17 | 4 | 2 |
| v2, ¼ epoch (iteration 560) | 18 | 4 | 1 |
| v2, ½ epoch (iteration 1120) | 11 | 3 | 0 |
| v2, 1 epoch (iteration 2240) | 9 | 4 | 0 |
| v1, 1 epoch | 8 | 4 | 0 |

The hedges are gone (v1 added one to 10 of 30 answers, v2 to none), but the
errors moved to garbled details: facts from different passages merged or
attributed to the wrong thing (q09, q15, q23, q24, q25, q27, q28).

**The more it trains, the worse it answers, while validation loss keeps
improving.** Validation loss measures how closely the model imitates Opus's
answers on Go/Rust/PEP passages: dense answers that combine several facts. A
0.6B model learns the density, not the reading, so it states more specifics and
gets more of them wrong. The refusal behaviour arrives early (¼ epoch) and holds.

Iteration 560 was picked here by the blog eval, not by validation loss, so its
18/30 is not a clean result: with 30 questions, 17 and 18 are the same score.
What it does show: a light fine-tune costs nothing on answerable questions and
may help a little on refusals; anything heavier hurts.

Conclusion: dataset v1/v2 doesn't teach Qwen3-0.6B to answer better. The
measurable win is the repetition penalty (13 → 17 for the base model). Options:

1. Stop fine-tuning. Re-run the blog's eval at penalty 1.0 for every model,
   including a check for looping (the reason the penalty exists), and change the
   blog.
2. One more dataset: regenerate only the teacher answers (the questions and
   passages are reused) in a style a 0.6B model can imitate: one fact, in the
   passage's own words. Answers only, through the Batch API: about $10. Pick the
   checkpoint on a judged dev set (the test split, judged by Opus, about $3 per
   checkpoint) instead of on validation loss, which this experiment shows is the
   wrong signal.

## v3: extractive teacher answers, checkpoints picked on judged answers

Only the teacher answers were regenerated (`generate --style extractive
--questions-from data/v1`; `claude.py: EXTRACTIVE_GUIDANCE`): one short sentence
with the fact asked for, in the passage's own words, no merging across passages,
no "the blog does not say..." clauses. Same questions and passages as v1. Teacher
cost $13.71 through the Batch API, with a 150-example pilot ($0.40) whose answers
were reused. Answers average 123 characters (v1: 180); 16% are refusals.

Qwen3-0.6B, one epoch (`scripts/train_v3.sh`, 1h18m). Validation loss 0.321,
0.308, 0.300, 0.298: lower than v1/v2 because short targets are easier to
predict, and not used for picking.

**Checkpoint selection** (`dev-prompts`, `dev-judge`): every checkpoint answers
the v3 test split (260 examples from documents not trained on), at repetition
penalty 1.0, and Opus grades the answers with the blog judge's rubric through the
Batch API (about $0.92 per checkpoint). The blog's 45 questions were run once, on
the checkpoint picked here.

| Qwen3-0.6B | answerable (214): faithful / unsupported / non-answer | not covered (46): correct / invented | faithful (of 260) |
|---|---|---|---|
| base | 102 / 85 / 27 | 15 / 27 | 117 |
| v3 ¼ epoch | 160 / 14 / 40 | 42 / 1 | 202 |
| v3 ½ epoch | 155 / 7 / 52 | 45 / 0 | 200 |
| v3 ¾ epoch | 158 / 16 / 40 | 44 / 0 | 202 |
| **v3 1 epoch** | **173** / 23 / 18 | 38 / 4 | **211** |

Picked: 1 epoch, on overall faithful answers.

**Blog eval** (45 questions, penalty 1.0, same export for every arm):

| Qwen3-0.6B | faithful (of 30) | unsupported | non-answer | invented (of 15) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| base | 17 | 9 | 4 | 2 |
| v1 | 8 | 18 | 4 | 0 |
| v2 | 9 | 17 | 4 | 0 |
| **v3** | **16** | **3** | 11 | **0** |

v3 is as faithful as the base model on the blog (16 vs 17 is noise) but rarely
misleads: 3 wrong answers on answerable questions and none on the 15 it shouldn't
answer, against 9 and 2. The cost is 11 answerable questions where it says the
blog doesn't cover it (or gives an off-target extract), against 4.

Those 11 are mostly broad questions: "What has the blog written about hashing
and bit arrays?", "...about running language models on your own hardware?",
"How do I build an AI that plays a monster-fighting game on its own?". The v3
questions are all specific facts from one chunk; the blog eval also has
navigational and conceptual questions. The base model got only 3 of the 11
right (q15, q20, q26); for the other 8 it was also unsupported or a non-answer.

**Conclusion.** v3 is the first fine-tune worth shipping: same hit rate, far
fewer wrong answers, and no invented answers to off-blog questions, which is what
"ask my blog" most needs. The gap is broad questions. Next options:

1. v4: add broad questions ("What has the blog written about X?", "How do I
   do Y?") to the question prompt, with answers that summarise what the passages
   say in one or two extractive sentences, and train on v3 + those.
2. Publish v3 (Hugging Face model repo + dataset) and offer it on /ask/ next to
   the base model.

Total spend for v3: $13.71 teacher, $4.69 dev judging (base + 4 checkpoints),
$0.45 blog judging.
